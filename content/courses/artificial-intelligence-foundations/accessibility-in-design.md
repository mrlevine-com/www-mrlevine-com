## Today's Objectives
- Articulate accessibility needs in prompts to fix issues.
- Identify accessibility issues in AI-generated layouts.

## Lesson Overview
### What is accessibility?
{{% define "Accessibility" %}}

**Nearly 95% of websites have accessibility issues**, like low-contrast text, images with no alt text describing them, and buttons with no label. AI learns from these websites, so it copies their issues. Your job as an inclusive designer is to catch and fix them.

### What are the POUR Principles?
{{% define "POUR Principles" %}}

| Principle | Users can... | Example issue |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|
| **Perceivable** | See or hear the content | Light gray text on a white background |
| **Operable** | Navigate and use the site | A button you can't reach with the Tab key |
| **Understandable** | Figure out what to do | An error message that only says "Invalid input" |
| **Robust** | Use any device or tool, like a screen reader | A form that gets cut off on a phone |

### How do you prompt AI to fix an issue?
Fix one issue per prompt. Name the issue, who it affects, and the fix you want:

| Vague | Specific |
|-------|----------|
| "Make my site accessible." | "The mountain photo has no alt text, so screen reader users can't tell what it shows (Perceivable). Add alt text that describes it, like 'snowy mountains at sunrise.'" |
