### How do brands use fonts and colors?
A brand designer makes sure a brand looks, feels, and communicates the same way everywhere a company shows up. They pick fonts and colors to attract a specific audience:

| Brand | Fonts and colors | Feels | Likely audience |
|-------|------------------|-------|-----------------|
| Nike | Bold letters, dark palette, high contrast | Powerful, competitive | Athletes |
| Spotify | Rounded letters, bright green | Youthful, approachable | Younger listeners |
| Luxury brands | Thin, elegant letters, muted colors with gold | Elegant, expensive | Adults with money to spend |

Go past "Nike uses black." Ask "What feeling does this palette send?" and "Who is this brand trying to attract?" Then flip it: "Who might feel left out by these choices?"

### What is a brand kit?
{{% define "Brand Kit" %}}

A brand kit has three parts:

- **Color palette**: 3–5 colors that set the mood
- **Typography**: a font pair, with one font for headings and one for body text
- **Visual style**: 1–2 words that describe the feel, like "calm" or "bold"

A strong brand kit doesn't just look good. It's:

- **Clear**: Text is easy to read, with strong contrast between text and background.
- **Consistent**: Every color and font feels like part of the same set.
- **Built for your user**: Each choice fits your user persona, not just your own taste.

Stuck? Pick one mood word for your user, then choose colors and fonts that match it. Browse free fonts at [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/).

### How do you explain your choices?
Every choice in your brand kit needs a reason tied to your user. Use these sentence starters:

- "We chose ___ because it communicates ___."
- "We chose ___ because it's easy to read and reflects ___."
- "This [color/font] feels ___ for our user persona while keeping strong contrast for readability."

If you only describe a color ("We picked blue"), ask yourself: "Why does that choice matter for my user?"

### Whose style does AI default to?
AI learns design from mainstream websites, so its suggestions lean toward the styles of the users who show up most in that data. Users outside that default might feel unseen or left out.

For example, a wellness brand's AI images might show only one body type, or a fashion brand's AI colors might come from one culture.

When the AI Brand Kit Helper suggests a change:

1. Ask, "Who is this choice designed for? Who might it not speak to?"
2. Keep only the suggestions that make your design better for your user.
