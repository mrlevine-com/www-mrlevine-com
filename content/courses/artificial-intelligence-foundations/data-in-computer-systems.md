### What is a sensor?
{{% define "Sensor" %}}

A sensor collects one or more types of data:

- **Text**: messages, transcripts, or signs
- **Audio**: recordings, noise levels, or speech
- **Visual**: photos, video, or thermal images
- **Biometric**: heart rate, oxygen levels, or other body signals
- **Location**: GPS coordinates
- **Environmental**: temperature, air quality, or humidity

Today, you'll research real sensors, then take on a career role and use data to solve a real-world problem.

#### Zookeeper
You're a zookeeper at Wildhaven Wildlife Sanctuary. Every nocturnal animal — fruit bats, lemurs, pangolins, fennec foxes — has turned sluggish overnight with limp wings and half-closed eyes, and your usual checks of diet, temperature, and habitat show nothing unusual.

#### Museum Curator
You manage the Grand Museum of Modern Art. Visitors keep reporting dizziness and headaches near a new glowing, moving sculpture called "Eternal Pulse," and some leave early or skip that room entirely.

#### Concert Promoter

#### Airplane Pilot
You're flying overnight from New York to London. Over the Atlantic your control panel flickers, your GPS coordinates disappear, your backup system briefly loses signal, and your radio turns unclear — with no bad weather to explain any of it.

#### Grocery Store Manager
You manage MetroMart. Energy drinks and protein bars keep vanishing from shelves that were fully stocked the night before, and security video shows nothing unusual.
