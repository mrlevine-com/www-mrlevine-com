### What makes one design stronger than another?
Strong designs fit their audience. Judge a design by asking about its:

- **Clarity**: Is it easy to read?
- **Cohesion**: Do the colors, fonts, and images feel consistent?
- **Audience fit**: Does the style match who the site is for?

Be specific when you name a strength or weakness:

| Vague | Specific |
|-------|----------|
| "Bad colors." | "Low contrast between text and background makes it hard to read." |
| "Looks better." | "One blue button stands out, so I know where to click." |

### What is a mood board?
{{% define "Mood Board" %}}

Designers use mood boards to explore and test a style before building anything final. It's like a Pinterest board, vision board, or outfit collage. A mood board includes:

- **Colors** that show your palette
- **Fonts** for headings and body text
- **Images** that capture the feeling of your brand
- **Logos and icons** that show its personality
- **2–3 style words** that describe the mood

For example, a mood board for a student cafe might use cream, brown, rust, and sage colors; photos of coffee and laptops; coffee cup icons; and the style words "Warm," "Creative," and "Inviting."

Pick each element on purpose. Ask yourself:

- "What feeling do I want my user to have?"
- "Do these colors and fonts work together as a set?"
- "If someone else saw this board, would they instantly get the vibe?"

Stuck? Ask the AI Design Help for ideas, then choose the ones that fit your style.

### How do you give helpful feedback?
Designers share drafts early so others can catch problems they can't see themselves. Good feedback is:

- **Specific**: It names one element, like a color, font, layout, or image.
- **Focused on the design**: It's about what the design communicates, not whether you personally like it.
- **Actionable**: It says what works or what could be stronger.

| Not helpful | Helpful |
|-------------|---------|
| "This looks cool." | "The high contrast makes this easy to read." |
