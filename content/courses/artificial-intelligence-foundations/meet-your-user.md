## Today's Objectives
- Create a persona representing a user type.
- Write a concise problem statement aligned to the persona.

## Lesson Overview
### Where do designers start?
**With a person and a problem, not features.** This is [human-centered design](/glossary/human-centered-design/): understand the person, define their need, *then* design a solution.

### How do you build a persona?
A persona is a [user profile](/glossary/user-profile/) for one specific, realistic person. Here's one for the scenario *keeping track of school club meetings*:

1. **Who are they?**
    - **Name**: Jordan
    - **Age or context**: 10th grade
    - **Role**: Member of robotics and student council
    - **Why it's hard**: Meeting updates come from too many group chats to keep up with.
2. **What are they going through?**
    - **Says**: "Wait, is that meeting today or tomorrow?"
    - **Thinks**: "I can't keep track of all these different group chats."
    - **Feels**: Overwhelmed, afraid of missing something
    - **Does**: Screenshots messages, checks multiple apps
3. **What's one frustration and one goal?**
    - **Frustration**: Meeting times are scattered across different platforms.
    - **Goal**: Wants one reliable place to see what's happening and when.

### What is a problem statement?
{{% define "Problem Statement" %}}

Combine your persona's frustration and goal into one sentence:

> **[User]** needs a way to **[need]** because **[reason]**.

- **Strong**: Jordan needs a way to see all club meeting updates in one place because juggling multiple group chats makes them miss important information.
- **Weak**: Jordan needs an app that sends notifications. *(It names a solution, not a need, and has no "because.")*

### What is decomposition?
{{% define "Decomposition" %}}

You just did it: you broke a messy, real person down into one specific problem you can design for.
