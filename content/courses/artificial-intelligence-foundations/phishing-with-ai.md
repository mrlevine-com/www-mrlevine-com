She tricked the cell phone company's worker, not its computers. In minutes, she got his email address, added herself to his account, and changed his password. She used three common tactics:

| Tactic        | What it does                                     | How she used it                                         |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| **Emotion**   | Fear, excitement, or sympathy lowers your guard  | She played a crying baby and kept apologizing           |
| **Urgency**   | Time pressure stops you from thinking it through | She said her husband needed it done today for a loan    |
| **Authority** | A believable claim makes you trust the caller    | She claimed to be the account holder's wife             |

### How does AI make these scams feel more real?
![Three AI scams. Smishing: a phone with a text bubble, where AI writes personal texts. Vishing: a phone with sound waves, where AI clones a voice. Deepfake: a video of a person, where AI fakes a face.](/images/ai-scam-channels.svg)

{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

AI makes them more realistic, more personal, and able to reach far more people.

| Scam         | How it reaches you      | How AI helps the attacker                                   |
|--------------|-------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Smishing** | Text message            | Writes messages with real names, friends, and recent events |
| **Vishing**  | Phone call or voicemail | Clones a real person's voice from a short recording         |
| **Deepfake** | Video or audio          | Fakes a real person's face and voice                        |

AI chatbots can also chat back in real time while pretending to be someone you trust. AI doesn't replace tactics like urgency; it makes them more convincing.

### How can you avoid falling for an AI scam?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

Pause, then check that it's real some other way. Mix both kinds of strategy:

- **Habits:** hang up and call the person back at a number you already know.
- **Tools:** turn on spam filters and two-factor login, so a stolen password isn't enough.

### How do you get through today's levels?

### Check Your Partner's Script (~15mins)
Check your partner's Level 5 script for red flags, and plan how to tell if it's real:

1. Sketch a phone screen playing your partner's script, with its most convincing line written on it.
2. Check off each red flag below that applies to the script. Add your own if you spot one.
3. Write one action next to each checked red flag that someone could take to tell whether the call or video is real.

| Red flag     | Ask yourself                                                   |
|--------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Sender**   | Do you recognize them? Is there a way to verify who they are?  |
| **Pressure** | Is it too good to be true, urgent, threatening, or emotional?  |
| **Links**    | Are there links or attachments?                                |
| **The ask**  | Do they want personal information or money?                    |
| **Errors**   | Are there mistakes in the message?                             |
