## Lesson Overview
### What is today's scenario?
Eight people answered a survey about their favorite cake flavor and favorite icing flavor.

| # | Cake Flavor | Icing Flavor |
|---|-------------|---------------|
| 1 | Chocolate Cake | Chocolate Icing |
| 2 | Red Velvet Cake | Cream Cheese Icing |
| 3 | Chocolate Cake | Chocolate Icing |
| 4 | Carrot Cake | Cream Cheese Icing |
| 5 | Carrot Cake | Vanilla Icing |
| 6 | Chocolate Cake | Chocolate Icing |
| 7 | Chocolate Cake | Cream Cheese Icing |
| 8 | Carrot Cake | Cream Cheese Icing |

If people had to agree on one cake and icing combination for a party, what should it be? Why?

### Why isn't it enough to look at cake flavor and icing flavor separately?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

Chocolate is the most popular cake flavor, and cream cheese is the most popular icing flavor, but only one person actually chose chocolate cake with cream cheese icing. Looking at the two answers on their own could lead you to recommend a combination that almost nobody actually wants.

Instead, you need to look at how the two answers relate to each other. Pairing chocolate cake with chocolate icing and carrot cake with cream cheese icing would make more people happy than pairing chocolate cake with cream cheese icing and carrot cake with chocolate icing, even though both pairs use the same four flavors.

{{</ collapse >}}

### What is cross tabulation?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

Cross tabulation is a way of counting how many people chose two things together, instead of just counting how many people chose each thing on its own. Instead of counting how many people like "dogs" and, separately, how many people like "camping," you count how many people like "dogs" and "camping" together. This helps you see how different answers relate to each other, so patterns like this can support a recommendation.

{{</ collapse >}}
