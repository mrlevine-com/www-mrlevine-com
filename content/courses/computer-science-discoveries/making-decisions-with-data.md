## Lesson Overview
### How can patterns in data help you make a decision?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

Looking at data by itself can make it hard to know what to do. Looking for patterns, like which choice comes up most often or how two pieces of data relate to each other, can point you toward a decision and give you evidence to support it.

Not every question has a clear "right answer." Sometimes patterns in data will point you toward collecting more data instead of making a decision right away.

{{</ collapse >}}

### Do two people need to make the same decision from the same data?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

No. Two people can look at the exact same data and reach different decisions, especially when the decision depends on what matters most to each person. Some data points clearly toward one choice, while other data is more open to interpretation.

{{</ collapse >}}

## Assignment
{{% instructions-unit-journal-update %}}

You'll look at three decisions. For each one, make your own decision based on the data before discussing it with your group, then record your decision, even if it's different from what your group decides.

### Decision 1: When to Post
**Define:** You want to know when to post a picture online to get the greatest response.

**Prepare:** To answer this question, you and your friends posted images to your favorite apps for a week. You kept track of likes and comments to see if there were any different patterns.

**Try:** You collected data about when you made a post and how many likes and comments you received. Average likes and comments for each time you posted are shown below.

![Bar chart of average likes and comments by time of day, Monday through Friday](/images/chart-when-to-post.png)

**Reflect:** Record your answers to the following questions:
- If you want the most likes, what time should you post?
- If you want the most comments, what time should you post?
- What other information would you want to collect to make an even better decision?

### Decision 2: Making Meals
**Define:** The cafeteria would like to decide how best to reduce their food waste after noticing they are throwing out food each day.

**Prepare:** To answer this question, the cafeteria decided to collect information about the number of meals prepared, daily attendance, the type of meal, and the number of meals eaten vs. wasted.

**Try:** The cafeteria collected this data and created the table below.

| | Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday | Thursday | Friday |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meal | Pizza | Hamburgers | Chicken Fingers | Salad | Grilled Cheese |
| Meals Prepared | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| Attendance | 85 | 90 | 90 | 95 | 85 |
| Meals Eaten | 80 | 82 | 88 | 75 | 80 |
| Meals Wasted | 20 | 18 | 12 | 25 | 20 |

**Reflect:** Record your answers to the following questions:
- How many meals should they prepare each day?
- Should they vary how many meals they make based on the meal served or attendance?
- Is there more information you'd want to collect to make your decision?

### Decision 3: Locker Selection
**Define:** Starting next year, your school has decided to let you choose your own locker so that it's the most convenient for your schedule. You need to make a data driven decision to decide which locker you're going to request.

**Prepare:** To answer this question, you and your friends collected data about how long it takes to walk from each locker to your classes by walking through the halls with a stopwatch.

**Try:** Here's the data you collected.

| | Time to 1st Period | Time to 2nd Period | Time to 3rd Period | Time to 4th Period | Time to 5th Period | Average Time (Go to locker every period) | Average Time (Skip locker on "Bad" periods) | Most Classes of Books You'll Carry if You Skip "Bad" Periods |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Locker A | 1 min (Good) | 2 mins (Good) | 5 mins (Bad) | 5 mins (Bad) | 1 min (Good) | 2.8 mins | 1.3 mins | 3 periods |
| Locker B | 2 mins (Good) | 3 mins (OK) | 2 mins (Good) | 4 mins (OK) | 3 mins (OK) | 2.8 mins | 2.8 mins | 1 period |
| Locker C | 6 mins (Bad) | 1 min (Good) | 6 mins (Bad) | 1 min (Good) | 6 mins (Bad) | 4.0 mins | 1 min | 2 periods |
| Locker D | 0 mins (Good) | 5 mins (Bad) | 5 mins (Bad) | 1 min (Good) | 1 min (Good) | 2.4 mins | 0.6 min | 3 periods |

**Reflect:** Record your answers to the following questions:
- Which locker do you want?
- Why? What data convinced you?
- Are there other pieces of data you'd also like to see before making a decision?
