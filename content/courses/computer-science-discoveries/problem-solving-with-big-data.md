## Lesson Overview
### What are some ways that apps, companies, or governments could be collecting data about you?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

In the last lesson, you saw how data could be collected through questions and used to make a recommendation. A survey that 10 or 20 people take is only a small part of the data being collected, though. Apps, websites, and devices all around you collect huge amounts of data every day, often while you're just using them normally.

Many companies, such as YouTube, Facebook, and Netflix, recommend videos and posts to you based on the data they've collected about you. Today you'll look at some real-world examples of how data is collected to solve problems.

{{</ collapse >}}

### What are the different ways data can be collected?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

In general, data can be collected in three ways:

- **User actively provided:** You provide data and understand that your actions are providing data to the company, like when you rate a Netflix video.
- **User passively provided:** You might not know that your behavior is generating data to solve a problem, like when Amazon records which products you look at.
- **Sensor data:** Sensors automatically collect data about the world, like when the Waze app collects GPS location data. Time, temperature, and location are all examples of data a computer can collect without anyone actively adding it. Usually sensor data isn't measuring anything about people directly.

The most important thing to realize is that you aren't always aware when you're producing data.
