## Lesson Overview
### What is today's scenario?
Pembroke Pines is planning a new building near Pembroke Lakes Mall, but the city council can't agree on what it should build. Some members want a fire station, others want an animal shelter, and others want a library. You've been hired as an expert to research real data about the area and use the problem solving process to make a convincing recommendation to the city council.

### How does the problem solving process apply to a data problem?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

You already know the four steps of the problem solving process: Define, Prepare, Try, and Reflect. When you use the process to solve a data problem, each step looks like this:

1. Define
    - What problem are you trying to solve?
2. Prepare
    - What data do you need to solve the problem?
    - Find that data and record what you find.
3. Try
    - Use the data you and your classmates collected to decide on a recommendation.
    - Support your recommendation with at least two types of data.
4. Reflect
    - What other data might help you make a better decision?
    - Where could you find that data?

### Prepare
What data do you need to solve your problem? Write down three types of data that might help you decide what the city needs most. If you're stuck, consider:
- What libraries, fire stations, or animal shelters are already in the area?
- Are people happy with the existing services?
- How many people live in the city, and how large is it?

With your group, find your data on the Internet and record what you find. You'll need to combine your data with data from other groups to make the best possible decision, so don't make a recommendation until you've heard from every group.
