## Today's Objectives
- Create a prototype of an animation or game design to meet the needs of a user using the problem-solving process.
- Identify features of an animation or game design that match the needs of users.
- Understand the steps of the problem-solving process.

## Lesson Overview
### What will you learn this quarter?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

You will build interactive animations and games.

Every animation or game starts with one question: who is it for? Sometimes you'll make one about your own interests. Other times, you'll design one for someone else. Today, you'll design for other people.

{{</ collapse >}}

### How do you apply the problem solving process to designing animations and games?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

1. Define
    - Decide who your animation or game is for.
    - Identify their needs and wants.
2. Prepare
    - Look at similar animations and games.
    - Brainstorm colors, images, and characters to use.
    - Discuss the pros and cons of your ideas.
3. Try
    - Draw your design on paper.
    - Create a basic version, even if it doesn't have every feature yet.
    - Share it with users for feedback.
4. Reflect
    - Review the feedback you got.
    - Improve your design.

The process is a cycle. When something doesn't work, go back to an earlier step and try again.

{{</ collapse >}}

### Design for a Client (~20mins)
Pick one client below. Then follow the problem solving process to design an animation or game for them.

| Client  | What They Want |
|---------|----------------|
| Tatiana | "I run a business that makes greeting cards and would like to have a digital version of my appreciation card. The card should have two characters, a cheese grater and a hunk of cheese, both with eyes and a smile. The words '_This might sound cheesy ..._' should be on the card. The user should be able to move the cheese left and right and when the cheese moves to be by the grate, the words '_But I think you're really grate!_' should appear. The card should also have instructions so the user knows to use arrow keys to move the cheese." |
| Daniel  | "I want to make a game for my grandma that is easy to play and stars her dog, a butterfly-chasing corgi. The game should include the corgi trying to catch butterflies. Her corgi doesn't like the color green so it would be fun if there were different green items like green apples or green veggies that the corgi had to avoid. It would be great if the game kept score too!" |

Complete the steps in order.

1. Define: Copy and fill in this sentence:
    > \_\_\_ needs a way to \_\_\_ because \_\_\_. It should \_\_\_.
2. Prepare: List your ideas for each of these:
    - Images to include
    - Colors to use
    - Where text goes on the screen
3. Try: Hand-draw a sketch of your design. Label the characters, text, and controls. It's a sketch, so don't try to make it perfect.
4. Share: Trade sketches with a classmate who picked the same client. Record one similarity and one difference between your designs.

### Reflect (~5mins)
Record your answers:

1. What is one thing your design does well to meet the needs of your user?
2. What is one area you can still improve to meet the needs of your user?
