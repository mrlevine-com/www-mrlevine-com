## Today's Objectives
- Choose and justify the use of different binary representation systems depending on the information being represented.
- Create a generalized representation system for many instances of a complex type of information.
- Encode and decode information represented in binary numbers and ASCII text.

## Lesson Overview
### What have I learned so far?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

You have learned how to represent:

- Numbers, using binary numbers
- Text, using ASCII
- Images, using pixels

Today, you will combine all of these systems to represent something more complex: a story about your perfect day.

## Assignment
{{% instructions-unit-journal-update %}}

### Step 1: Describe Your Perfect Day (Day 1)
Write a 1 paragraph description of your perfect day. Think about: What is the weather like? What will you do? Who's there with you?

### Step 2: Share with a Partner (Day 1)
Switch your description with a classmate. Read through your partner's description, and update your own description with any new details you think of.

### Step 3: Choose Representations (Day 1)
Sort the information in your description into three categories:

- **Numbers** (e.g. temperature, # of people present, when you wake up)
- **Characters** (e.g. location name, type of weather, type of activity)
- **Images** (e.g. what the location, activity, or people look like)

### Step 4: Review Information Choices (Day 1)
Meet with your partner and compare how you each sorted your information. Discuss:

- Whether you think they missed any important information
- What types of information you both have in common

### Step 5: Agree on a Classwide Punch Card (Day 1)
As a class, agree on how to use a punch card with 3 numbers, 10 characters, and a single 8x8 image to represent everyone's perfect day. Record what information each row will represent, e.g.:

- Number 1: Temperature that day
- Number 2: How many other people are there
- Number 3: How long you're there, in hours
- Characters 1-10: Name of activity or location
- Image: Image of the activity

A representation system is only useful if it works for everyone, not just one person. Be ready to discuss the tradeoffs your class's system makes.

### Step 6: Fill Out Your Punch Card (Day 2)
Using your class's punch card structure, fill out your own punch card.

### Step 7: Trade and Decode Punch Cards (Day 2)
Give your punch card to a classmate you have not worked with so far. Don't tell them anything about your perfect day. Decode your classmate's punch card and record:

- The numbers you decoded and what they represent
- The characters you decoded and what they represent
- What you believe the image is showing
- Your best description of your classmate's perfect day, based only on what you decoded

### Step 8: Share and Reflect (Day 2)
Meet back up with your partner and compare their description of your perfect day to what you actually intended. Record your answers:

- What information was your partner still able to capture?
- What information was lost?
- Why were you unable to represent that information?
