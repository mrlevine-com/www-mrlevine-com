## Lesson Overview
### What is today's scenario?
The local pizza parlor wants to donate a giant one-topping pizza to your class for your end-of-unit pizza party, and your class has to decide what topping to order. Your choices are:

- Pepperoni
- Sausage
- Barbecue Chicken
- Green Peppers
- Mushrooms
- Pineapple
- Extra Cheese

You'll turn your class's votes into a bar chart, then decide what to do with votes that don't fit neatly into one of these choices.

### Why do people and computers understand data differently?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

People usually understand data best when it's turned into a visual, like a bar chart, while computers understand data best when it's organized into clean categories and numbers, like a table.

Sometimes the raw data (the way the information is first collected) needs to be cleaned (put into a different form) so that both people and computers can understand what it means.

{{</ collapse >}}

### Why must data be cleaned before a computer can use it?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

A computer will use every piece of data exactly as it was entered, even data that's misspelled, capitalized differently, or completely irrelevant. It can't tell that "Cheese" and "cheese" mean the same thing, that "peppers" probably means "green peppers," or that "I will be absent :(" isn't a real vote at all.

Because a computer doesn't actually understand what the data means, people have to clean the data before a computer can use it, or collect the data in a way that keeps it clean from the start.

{{</ collapse >}}
