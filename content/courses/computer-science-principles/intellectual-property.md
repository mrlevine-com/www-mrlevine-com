## Lesson Overview
### How would you feel if someone took an image you posted online for your friends to see, put it on a T-shirt, and sold it for $10 a piece??!!
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

You would feel terrible!! Or... you wouldn't care.

Nonetheless, when you create materials on the computer, you own them; they are your "Intellectual Property".

{{% define "Intellectual Property" %}}

Using materials created by someone else and trying to pass them off as your own is plagiarism when you don't have the creator's permission. There may even be legal consequences for using these materials!

{{</ collapse >}}

### What if I want others to be able to freely use my copyrighted materials?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

You can license it under a Creative Commons license. Check out the Creative Commons notice at the bottom of this page!

{{% define "Creative Commons" %}}

There are a few other licenses that you may have heard about that also allow access:

- Open Source: programs that are made freely available and may be redistributed and modified.
- Open Access: online research output free of restrictions to access and use

{{</ collapse >}}
