## Lesson Overview
### What's the difference between what a chart shows and why?
{{< figure src="/images/data-story-fast-food.svg" alt="A line graph of change since 2000. Fast-food restaurants rose about 40%. Teens ages 16-19 rose about 5%. Teens with jobs fell about 30%." caption="Source: The New York Times, \"A Fast-Food Problem: Where Have All the Teenagers Gone?\" (2018), from [What's Going On in This Graph?](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/06/10/learning/over-60-new-york-times-graphs-for-students-to-analyze.html) Redrawn, so values are approximate." >}}

{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

The "what" is a fact. The "why" is a guess.

- **What:** Fewer teens have jobs than in 2000, even though there are more fast-food restaurants.
- **Why:** Maybe more teens are focusing on school.
- Your "why" could be wrong, so always keep it separate from the "what."

{{</ collapse >}}

### If two lines match, did one cause the other?
{{< figure src="/images/correlation-margarine.svg" alt="Two lines from 2000 to 2009, margarine eaten in the U.S. and divorces in Maine, falling in almost the same pattern." caption="Source: [Spurious Correlations](https://www.tylervigen.com/spurious/correlation/5920_per-capita-consumption-of-margarine_correlates-with_the-divorce-rate-in-maine), using data from the U.S. Department of Agriculture and the CDC." >}}

{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

No. **Correlation does not equal causation.**

- **Correlation:** two things follow the same pattern.
- **Causation:** one thing makes the other happen.
- Eating margarine didn't cause divorces in Maine. The lines just match.

{{</ collapse >}}

### What is metadata?
![A photo with an arrow pointing to its metadata: camera, date taken, size, and downloads.](/images/metadata-photo.svg)

{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

Data about data. It's like a label that tells you about the data.

- It tells you where data came from, so you know if you can trust it.
- It helps you find and organize data.
- Changing it doesn't change the data itself.
