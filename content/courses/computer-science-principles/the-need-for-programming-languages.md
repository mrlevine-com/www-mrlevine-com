## Today's Objectives
- Explain the qualities that differentiate natural languages and programming languages.
- Justify the existence of programming languages to precisely communicate instructions.

## Lesson Overview
### Why is it so hard to write clear instructions?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

You use instructions all the time, whether it's directions to get somewhere, instructions to fill out a form, or instructions from your teachers. Even so, instructions that seem clear often turn out to be vague once someone actually tries to follow them.

You could improve your instructions by being more careful or adding more detail, but the core challenge is that everyday human language, called natural language, is **ambiguous**. A single word can have two or three meanings, and a sentence can be read in more than one way. When you struggle to write clear instructions, it's not entirely your fault: it's hard to do a good job with bad tools!

{{</ collapse >}}

### Why do programming languages exist?
{{< collapse summary="Click here to reveal the answer." >}}

When you write a program, you're giving a computer instructions for what it should do to run your app. A computer follows those instructions exactly, so it needs a language that leaves no room for interpretation.

A **programming language** is designed to be more structured and precise than natural language, so that instructions written in it can be followed correctly 100% of the time. The programming language you'll use sometimes looks like English, but it has a lot of differences that might seem weird or confusing at first. Those differences exist because it needs to be more precise and unambiguous than normal human language.

{{</ collapse >}}
